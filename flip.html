Why this always has one exact answer

Call white 0 and black 1, and add colors mod 2 (mod 2 arithmetic: it wraps around every 2 steps, so 1+1=0). Flipping a 2×2 block adds 1 to each of its four cells. Flip the same block twice and you're back where you started — a move undoes itself, so order never matters and no dot is ever worth pressing twice.

There are 9 possible blocks — one centered on each interior grid-line intersection. Each is a vector (a list of 16 numbers mod 2, one per cell) recording which cells it touches. Those 9 vectors turn out to be linearly independent — mathematician's phrase for "no smaller combination of them cancels out to all-zero." That means all 2⁹ = 512 subsets of moves land on 512 different boards, and since the map is invertible, every one of those boards has exactly one subset of moves that solves it. That's the whole trick: a unique shortest solution, and a hint button that never has to guess.
